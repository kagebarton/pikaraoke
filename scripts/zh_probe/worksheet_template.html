__TITLE__

A program will time every Chinese character of these __COUNT_WORD__ songs to the singing. To do that it needs one exact lyric text per song, and the lyric websites disagree with each other. Please turn each text below into the words the singer actually sings in the linked recording.

How this works

For each song

  1. Open the recording on YouTube and keep it in another tab. The button is at the top of each song.
  2. Start from any version (A, B, C ...). They all differ only in small ways, and the list of spots shows every place they disagree.
  3. For each spot, press its play link, listen, and tick the wording you hear. Tick "Can't tell" if you can't hear it. A tick only records what you heard; it does not change your text.
  4. Make your text say what you ticked. If it doesn't yet, a warning shows under that spot and goes away once the text matches. If the right wording is neither choice, tick "Neither" and fix the text.
  5. Listen through once more, edit the text until it matches the singing from the first line to the last, then tick Done.

When you are finished

Nothing is sent from this page. Open Send back, press Copy all, and paste what it copies into a message to the person who sent you this link. If copying does not work, use Download .txt and attach the file.

What a finished sheet looks like

  • One sung phrase per line, the way a karaoke screen would show it.
  • Every repeat written out. A chorus sung three times appears three times.
  • No credits (词, 曲), no titles, no section names such as [Verse].
  • Leave instrumental breaks out. Do not write notes or markers such as "(music break)" inside the sheet. Use the notes box for anything you want to tell us.
  • Keep the script (Simplified or Traditional) of the version you started from. Do not convert it.
  • Keep English words as sung. Short sung syllables such as 喔 count if you hear them clearly. Leave out breaths and humming.
  • When two wordings sound the same (那/哪, 以/已), pick the one that is right for the sentence.
  • Cantonese songs: keep the Cantonese wording as sung, not a Mandarin rewrite.
